# Ranked Problem-Statement Ideas for a 2026 Agentic-Workflows Hackathon

## TL;DR
- The strongest hackathon problems in 2026 share four traits judges reward: a named persona with a documented, expensive bottleneck; a fair baseline (the current manual process or a single LLM prompt) that can be beaten on a measurable metric; genuine multi-step agentic engineering (retrieval, tool use, memory, verification loops, orchestration, human checkpoints) rather than a prompt wrapper; and clean reproducibility from public/synthetic data. My top three recommendations that best satisfy all of these are (1) an **evidence-screening agent for systematic literature reviews**, (2) a **vulnerability (CVE) triage-and-remediation-planning agent**, and (3) a **security-questionnaire / RFP response agent**.
- These three win on the hardest-to-fake rubric line, **Measured Improvement (15 pts)**: each has objective, publicly available ground truth (labeled inclusion decisions for reviews; the CISA KEV catalog and EPSS for exploitation risk; a company's own prior answered questionnaires) so a baseline-vs-agent comparison is genuinely fair rather than a vibes-based demo.
- Avoid the failure modes judges call out most: "too generic" (frontier models can't tell that dozens of teams built the identical LangChain+Pinecone RAG demo), "no real baseline" (only ~39% of production agent teams even compare against a non-agentic baseline), "thin agentic design" (an LLM with extra steps), and "not reproducible" (works only after an undocumented multi-hour agent chat). Pick one narrow persona, instrument a changelog of iterations, and containerize the environment.

## Key Findings

**What judges at recent agent hackathons actually reward.** Across the Microsoft AI Agents Hackathon 2025 (18,000+ registrants, 570 submissions), the Kong Agentic AI Hackathon 2025, the NVIDIA NeMo Agent Toolkit Hackathon, and the Agno Global Agent Hackathon, the recurring winning pattern is: (a) a concrete real-world problem, (b) a working demo with real code, and (c) systems that "observe, decide, and act" rather than passively answer. Kong's organizers explicitly said winners "solved real-world problems," "built systems that take initiative," and "shipped" a working demo. AngelHack's judging guidance is blunt: "Judges look for a clear problem, a working demo, and evidence that the AI improves the result. A focused project that works will beat an ambitious one that only half-works." [AngelHack](https://angelhack.com/blog/ai-hackathon-ideas-for-developer/)

**What gets weak submissions dinged.** Experienced judges report being unimpressed by "tech for tech's sake" and flashy demos with weak problem statements. An analysis of AI-judged hackathon submissions found frontier models rate almost every project 8–10/10 on "innovation" because they can't tell that dozens of teams built the identical "LangChain + Pinecone RAG system." A professional agent-rubric evaluator called reproducibility "the silent killer": "If a judge can't clone your repo and get the same result, correctness is unverifiable — and unverifiable correctness scores as zero, not partial credit." A study of agents in production found only 39% of teams compare their agent to a non-agentic baseline and 26% say no meaningful baseline exists — so a rigorous baseline is itself a differentiator. AngelHack's 2026 playbook warns that "agents without an environment are LLMs with extra steps" [AngelHack](https://angelhack.com/blog/ai-agent-hackathon/) and that without an evaluation framework "judges score videos and vibes."

**The 2026 macro-context.** Enterprise reports converge on the same theme: model capability is no longer the bottleneck; access to trusted context, integration, and verification is. Gartner's 2026 CIO and Technology Executive Survey found only 17% of organizations have deployed AI agents so far, but 42% expect to do so within the next 12 months and another 22% within the following year — Gartner's most aggressive adoption curve of any emerging technology measured. In an August 26, 2025 press release, Gartner Senior Director Analyst Anushree Verma projected that "forty percent of enterprise applications will be integrated with task-specific AI agents by the end of 2026, up from less than 5% today." Gartner has also warned (June 25, 2025 press release, based on a January 2025 poll of 3,412 webinar attendees) that "over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027, due to escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls." The takeaway for a submission: demonstrating verification, auditability, and human-in-the-loop control is aligned with where the market says the real problems are.

**Well-documented bottleneck categories with hard evidence.** My research surfaced strong, quantified evidence in each of these domains:
- **Compliance/audit evidence collection:** Hyperproof's 2023 IT Compliance Benchmark found compliance pros spend 38% of their time on manual tasks; one analysis models manual evidence collection at 200–400 hours per audit cycle for small teams. [Blacksmithinfosec](https://blacksmithinfosec.com/8-msp-compliance-bottlenecks-to-fix-before-you-scale/)
- **Contract review:** The average contract review cycle is 19 days (World Commerce & Contracting); in-house counsel spend ~3.2 hours per contract (EY); SpotDraft's 2025 survey of 115 in-house teams found 49% still manage contracts via email/Word/shared folders and estimated automation could cut processing from 19 days to 3.
- **Financial close/reconciliation:** Ledge's 2025 Month-End Close Benchmarks found cash reconciliation consumes 20–50 hours per month and is the single biggest close bottleneck.
- **Security questionnaires/RFPs:** Per Iris AI's 2026 analysis, "88% of organizations take over two weeks to complete vendor assessments using manual methods," and 54% of companies report losing deals for failing to complete questionnaires on time; teams spend 5–15 hours per questionnaire.
- **Systematic literature review:** Borah, Brown, Capers & Kaiser (BMJ Open 2017, a PROSPERO registry analysis) found "the mean estimated time to complete the project and publish the review was 67.3 weeks (IQR=42)," with a mean included-study yield rate of 2.94%; title/abstract screening alone averages 33 days, with human screening error rates of 6–21%.
- **DevOps incident response / alerts:** An incident.io 2025 survey of hundreds of DevOps/SRE teams found "85% of teams report that the majority of their alerts are false positives" and "67% of engineers admit to ignoring or dismissing alerts without investigating"; one team reported 2,000+ alerts/week with only 3% requiring action. AI incident automation is cited as cutting MTTR by ~40%.
- **Vulnerability triage:** CVE volume overwhelms analysts; CVSS alone doesn't prescribe action, and NIST's 2026 NVD enrichment overhaul means many CVEs will lack scores, pushing triage burden onto teams.
- **Customer support triage:** Misrouting affects ~30% of tickets and manual classification runs 60–70% accuracy; agent turnover ~40%.
- **QA/flaky tests:** Engineers spend ~30 min per flaky-test investigation; modeled cost ~$120,000/year for a 20-engineer team.
- **Prior authorization / claims appeals (healthcare):** The 2024 AMA Prior Authorization Physician Survey (1,000 physicians, published December 2024) found physicians spend ~13 hours/week handling an average of 39 prior authorizations per week; per AMA, 83.2% of appealed denials "resulted in the insurance company either partially or fully overturning the initial prior authorization denial in 2022," yet only ~11% of denials are appealed — a huge, evidence-backed gap.

**Reproducible public/synthetic data exists for the best ideas.** This was the thinnest part of my own sourcing, so I verified concrete named datasets:
- Contract review: **CUAD (Contract Understanding Atticus Dataset) v1** — 510 commercial contracts, 13,000+ expert annotations across 41 clause categories, sourced from SEC EDGAR, CC BY 4.0, on HuggingFace/GitHub.
- Literature-review screening: **SYNERGY** (169,288 works from 26 systematic reviews, only 1.67% relevant — sparse labels, CC BY 4.0) and **CLEF TAR 2017–2019** (Cochrane-based, PMIDs + relevance labels).
- Support triage: **Bitext Customer Support** (fully synthetic, ~27,000 Q/A pairs, 27 intents) and the **Ubuntu Dialogue Corpus** (~1M dialogues, anonymized).
- Financial analysis: **SEC EDGAR Financial Statement Data Sets** (all XBRL numeric facts, SUB/TAG/NUM/PRE tables); note there is no standard public transaction-level reconciliation dataset, so synthetic ledgers are needed.
- Incident/log triage: **Loghub / LogPAI** (19 real-world log datasets; Loghub-2.0 averages ~3.69M logs each) plus curated public postmortem corpora (danluu/post-mortems).
- Vulnerability triage: **NVD CVE API v2.0**, **CISA KEV catalog** (free JSON feed of exploited-in-the-wild CVEs), and **FIRST EPSS** (daily exploitation-probability scores) — all free and machine-readable.

## Details: Ranked Shortlist of Problem-Statement Candidates

Below are eight diverse, generalizable candidates ranked by overall fit to this rubric (problem value + agentic depth + fair measurability + reproducibility), with the top three developed in depth. None overlaps with the organizers' three appendix examples (repo-quality assessment, recruiter candidate synthesis, podcast translation consistency).

### #1 — Evidence-screening agent for systematic literature reviews
- **Persona & current workflow:** A researcher, grad student, or evidence-synthesis analyst (medicine, social science, ML) who must screen thousands of titles/abstracts against inclusion/exclusion criteria. Today two humans independently screen every abstract with a third resolving conflicts; this averages 33 days for screening and contributes to a mean 67.3-week total review.
- **Bottleneck evidence:** 67.3 weeks average per review (Borah et al., BMJ Open 2017); 6–21% human error rates; screening is repeatedly named the most time-consuming, error-prone stage.
- **Fair baseline:** (a) the manual double-screen, and (b) a single-prompt classifier ("Given these criteria, include/exclude this abstract"). Both are trivial to implement and compare.
- **Stronger agentic solution:** An active-learning loop (rank remaining abstracts by relevance, ask the human to label the most informative, update), retrieval of the full protocol and prior inclusion decisions as memory, a verification agent that must cite which inclusion criterion each decision hinges on, and a conflict-resolution sub-agent that flags borderline items for human review. Human-in-the-loop is native to the domain.
- **Primary success metric:** **WSS@95** (Work Saved over Sampling at 95% recall) — the field's standard metric — plus recall of known-relevant papers. Because SYNERGY and CLEF TAR ship ground-truth labels, this is fully objective.
- **Why it ranks #1:** Best measurability of any candidate (real labels, a canonical metric), a legitimately hard baseline to beat, obvious agentic structure (memory + active learning + verification), and clean public data. This also directly generalizes to any organization doing due-diligence document triage.
- **Hot-take/failure mode to hunt for:** Active-learning agents tend to over-exploit early patterns and miss "concept-drift" relevant papers that use different terminology — a demonstrable failure where recall collapses on a held-out cluster, motivating a diversity-sampling fix.

### #2 — Vulnerability (CVE) triage & remediation-planning agent
- **Persona & current workflow:** A SOC analyst or security engineer facing a daily flood of CVEs who must decide what to patch first. Today they eyeball CVSS scores, manually cross-reference exploitation and asset exposure, and route tickets — slow and inconsistent, especially now that NIST's 2026 NVD overhaul leaves many CVEs unscored.
- **Bottleneck evidence:** CVSS "does not prescribe tailored remediation actions"; analysts fall back on arbitrary >7.0 cutoffs; NVD enrichment gaps create "context debt."
- **Fair baseline:** Sort-by-CVSS (the current default) and a single-prompt "rank these CVEs" call.
- **Stronger agentic solution:** A multi-tool orchestration that enriches each CVE by calling NVD, CISA KEV, and EPSS; correlates against a (synthetic) asset inventory for blast-radius; applies an SSVC-style decision framework; writes an auditable rationale per decision; and requires human confirmation before any CVE becomes "priority-one." Verification loop: the agent must justify each ranking against retrieved evidence and abstain when confidence is low.
- **Primary success metric:** Ranking quality (nDCG / precision@k) measured against a ground-truth "should-have-been-urgent" set derived from the CISA KEV catalog (CVEs later confirmed exploited in the wild) — an objective, time-separated label.
- **Why it ranks #2:** Unbeatable public data (NVD + KEV + EPSS are all free, live, machine-readable), a naturally multi-step tool-use/orchestration design, and a defensible ground-truth signal. Generalizes to any risk-prioritization queue.
- **Hot-take/failure mode:** Agents anchor on CVSS severity and under-weight EPSS, so they systematically deprioritize low-CVSS-but-actively-exploited CVEs — exactly the ones KEV proves matter. A great changelog story.

### #3 — Security-questionnaire / RFP response agent
- **Persona & current workflow:** A GRC/security engineer (or founder at an early-stage vendor) who must answer lengthy customer security questionnaires (SOC 2, ISO 27001, SIG, CAIQ) to unblock sales. Today they copy-paste from prior answers scattered across docs, spreadsheets, and inboxes.
- **Bottleneck evidence:** Per Iris AI's 2026 analysis, 88% of organizations take over two weeks per assessment and 54% report losing deals for missing questionnaire deadlines; teams spend 5–15 hours each; inconsistent answers "erode buyer trust." This is explicitly described as "the biggest bottleneck in the enterprise sales cycle." [Whisperly](https://whisperly.ai/rfp-automation)
- **Fair baseline:** Keyword search over a past-answers spreadsheet, plus a single-prompt "answer this question from our policy doc."
- **Stronger agentic solution:** Retrieval over a synthetic policy/evidence corpus and a memory of previously approved answers (so answers stay consistent — a natural memory story), per-answer confidence scoring, a verification agent that checks each drafted answer against the cited source and flags contradictions with prior answers, and a human-approval checkpoint before "sending."
- **Primary success metric:** Answer accuracy and consistency vs. a held-out gold set of previously approved answers, plus % of questions answered without human intervention at a fixed accuracy threshold.
- **Why it ranks #3:** Strong, well-evidenced business value; memory/consistency is the crux (mirrors the "podcast consistency" spirit the organizers liked, but in a distinct, high-value B2B domain); human-in-the-loop is native. Slightly lower than #1–2 on reproducibility because the corpus must be synthetic (real answers are private), but that is fully doable.
- **Hot-take/failure mode:** The agent confidently reuses a stale approved answer after a policy change, propagating a now-false claim — motivating a "freshness/verification" check as the key insight.

### #4 — Contract risk-review agent (CUAD)
- **Persona:** In-house counsel or procurement reviewer. **Bottleneck:** 19-day cycles, 3.2 hrs/contract, 49% still on email/Word. **Baseline:** single-prompt "find risky clauses." **Agentic:** clause-by-clause retrieval, a playbook of the org's standard positions as memory, a verifier that must locate the exact clause span, and severity scoring. **Metric:** clause-extraction precision/recall against **CUAD's 41 labeled categories** — objective ground truth. **Failure mode:** over-flagging boilerplate as high-risk (low precision) once you leave the clause types seen in the playbook. Ranks just below the top three mainly because it's a crowded commercial space, but rubric fit is excellent thanks to CUAD's labels.

### #5 — DevOps incident triage & root-cause-hypothesis agent
- **Persona:** On-call SRE. **Bottleneck:** 85% of alerts are false positives; ~2,000 alerts/week, ~3% actionable; MTTR pain. **Baseline:** severity-threshold alerting + single-prompt summary. **Agentic:** correlate/deduplicate alerts, retrieve relevant runbooks and past postmortems (memory), form ranked root-cause hypotheses with evidence, draft the incident timeline, and propose (never auto-execute) remediation with human approval. **Data:** Loghub/LogPAI logs + public postmortem corpora. **Metric:** correct root-cause identification and alert-noise reduction on replayed labeled incidents. **Failure mode:** the agent latches onto the loudest alert (symptom) instead of the causal one. Sandbox/human-approval is essential and natural here.

### #6 — Customer-support ticket triage + resolution-draft agent
- **Persona:** Support team lead. **Bottleneck:** ~30% misrouting, 60–70% manual classification accuracy, chronic backlogs. **Baseline:** rules/keyword routing + single-prompt classifier. **Agentic:** classify + route by skill, retrieve KB articles and similar past tickets (memory), draft a grounded resolution with citations, verify against policy, and escalate low-confidence/sensitive tickets to a human. **Data:** Bitext (synthetic) + Ubuntu Dialogue Corpus. **Metric:** routing accuracy and draft-resolution quality vs. labeled intents. **Failure mode:** confident wrong routing on multi-intent tickets. Common category, so novelty of framing matters.

### #7 — Financial month-end reconciliation agent
- **Persona:** Staff accountant / controller. **Bottleneck:** cash reconciliation 20–50 hrs/month, the top close bottleneck. **Baseline:** spreadsheet matching + single-prompt "match these transactions." **Agentic:** multi-source ingestion, fuzzy transaction matching, exception investigation with tool calls, a verifier that must explain each proposed match, and a human sign-off on adjustments. **Data:** synthetic ledgers + SEC EDGAR statement data (note: no standard public transaction-level reconciliation dataset, a reproducibility caveat). **Metric:** match precision/recall and exceptions correctly surfaced on a synthetic ground-truth ledger. **Failure mode:** silently auto-matching a coincidental amount, hiding a real discrepancy.

### #8 — Flaky-test triage agent
- **Persona:** QA engineer / release manager. **Bottleneck:** ~30 min per flaky-test investigation; ~$120k/year for a 20-engineer team; erodes trust in CI. **Baseline:** rerun-and-hope + single-prompt "is this flaky?" **Agentic:** cluster failures, re-run selectively, compare against recent passing runs, retrieve recent diffs to judge whether the touched code could explain the failure, classify real-regression vs. flake with evidence, and quarantine with human confirmation. **Data:** public CI logs / synthetic failure traces. **Metric:** flake-vs-regression classification accuracy on labeled runs. **Failure mode:** quarantining a genuinely broken test as "flaky," letting a real regression ship — a compelling hot-take.

**Honorable mentions (viable but slightly weaker on one axis):** a nonprofit grant-application drafting/compliance agent (30–120 hrs per application; weaker objective ground truth) and a prior-authorization appeal-letter agent (13 hrs/week physician burden, 83.2% appeal-success rate; strong value but sensitive data and harder fair-metric design).

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Choose from the top three.** Pick #1 (literature-review screening) if you want the cleanest, most defensible **Measured Improvement** score; #2 (CVE triage) if you want the strongest public-data story and richest tool-orchestration; #3 (security questionnaire) if you want the most obvious business value and a memory-centric design. All three avoid overlap with the appendix examples and score well across every rubric line.

**Stage 2 — Engineer for the 30-point "Agent Solution" line, not the demo.** Deliberately include at least four agentic capabilities and make each visible in a trace log: (a) tool use / retrieval, (b) memory of prior decisions, (c) a verification/self-correction loop that can abstain, and (d) a human-approval checkpoint before any consequential action (kept in a sandbox/simulation per the ground rules). Judges in 2026 increasingly score reasoning traces; expose them.

**Stage 3 — Build the baseline first and keep a changelog.** Implement the single-prompt baseline and the manual-process estimate on day one, freeze an evaluation harness, and record every iteration ("added verification loop → precision +9 pts"). This directly earns the Measured Improvement points and answers the "no real baseline" criticism.

**Stage 4 — Make it reproducible from a clean environment.** Use one of the named public/synthetic datasets, pin dependencies, commit a lockfile, containerize, and provide a single setup command plus a sample outputs folder so judges can evaluate without a full run. Treat "works only after an undocumented agent chat" as an automatic zero.

**Stage 5 — Prepare the 5-point hot take.** Instrument for the specific failure mode listed with each idea (e.g., anchoring on CVSS over EPSS; reusing stale approved answers; quarantining a real regression). Show the failure, then the fix, in your changelog — this is the highest-ROI 5 points in the rubric because almost no one does it well.

**Benchmarks that would change the recommendation:** If your team lacks security/domain familiarity, drop #2/#4 (they require correct domain framing to avoid nonsense outputs) and prefer #1 or #6. If you cannot secure any clean labeled data, avoid #7 (no public reconciliation ground truth) and pick #1, #2, or #4, which have labeled public datasets.

## Caveats
- Several bottleneck statistics come from vendors selling the corresponding automation (e.g., reconciliation, contract, and questionnaire tools), which have an incentive to inflate pain and savings figures; I prioritized survey- and benchmark-based numbers (AMA, Gartner, Hyperproof, SpotDraft, incident.io, Borah et al./BMJ Open) where possible and flagged the rest. Treat single-vendor "X% faster" claims as directional.
- Forward-looking figures (Gartner's 40%-by-2026 integration projection, "40% of agentic projects will be canceled by 2027") are projections, not realized outcomes, and are labeled as such. The Gartner cancellation figure derives from a January 2025 poll of 3,412 webinar attendees rather than a controlled study.
- Dataset specifics were verified via a targeted secondary search; the frequently cited "67.3 weeks" systematic-review figure traces to Borah, Brown, Capers & Kaiser (BMJ Open 2017) and is widely reused. There is no standard public transaction-level financial-reconciliation dataset, so idea #7's reproducibility depends on synthesizing ledgers.
- This report ranks *fit to the stated rubric*, not commercial novelty; #4, #6, and #7 are crowded commercial spaces, so a winning submission there must lead with a sharp persona and a distinctive agentic design rather than the idea alone.
- I could not confirm by name the "Cambridge/8600-review" screening dataset; SYNERGY and CLEF TAR are the well-established, verifiable public alternatives.
